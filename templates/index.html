STREAM TAXONOMY

THE 5 CORE WASTE CATEGORIES

Every discarded item belongs to a specific stream. Understanding bin colors and material traits prevents recycling contamination.

GREEN BIN Biodegradable

Wet & Organic Waste

Moisture-rich organic matter that naturally decomposes into nutrient-rich compost or bio-gas.

Common Examples: Vegetable peels, fruit cores (apple, banana), cooked food leftovers, coffee grounds, eggshells, garden clippings.
BLUE BIN Recyclable

Dry & Recyclable Waste

Inorganic materials that can be mechanically melted, remanufactured, or re-pulped into new goods.

Common Examples: PET water bottles, soda cans (aluminum), cardboard cartons, clean paper, tin boxes, glass jars. Must be rinsed & dry!
ORANGE / RED BIN Special Handling

Electronic Waste (E-Waste)

Discarded electrical or electronic equipment containing printed circuit boards, precious metals, and toxic heavy elements.

Common Examples: Laptops, smartphones, charging cables, headphones, circuit boards, computer peripherals. Never toss in regular bins!

THE 5 RS OF WASTE MANAGEMENT

Recycling is only the final safety net. Follow the hierarchy of circular living from top to bottom.

1. REFUSE FIRST LINE OF DEFENSE

Politely decline non-essential single-use plastics, disposable plastic cutlery, flyers, extra carrier bags, and double-wrapped products. The greenest waste is the waste that was never created in the first place.

2. REDUCE CONSCIOUS CONSUMPTION

Buy in bulk to cut packaging ratios, choose durable goods over cheap disposables, digitalize receipts and bills, and plan weekly groceries strictly to prevent kitchen spoilage.

3. REUSE EXTEND LIFECYCLE

Wash and reuse glass jars for kitchen pantry storage, carry stainless-steel water bottles, maintain canvas shopping bags, and repair broken appliances rather than immediately discarding them.

4. REPURPOSE CREATIVE UPCYCLING

Give expired goods a second life: convert plastic jugs into seed-starting planters, turn worn towels into kitchen cleaning rags, and reuse delivery boxes for home organization.

5. RECYCLE MATERIAL LOOP CLOSURE

When an item can no longer be reused, ensure it is thoroughly cleaned, dried, and sorted into municipal or specialized streams so raw metals, paper pulps, and polymers can be mechanically remanufactured.

6 COSTLY SEGREGATION MISTAKES

Even well-intentioned citizens frequently make these six errors that derail recycling operations.

MISTAKE 01

Tossing Unwashed Food Containers

Leaving peanut butter, yogurt, or salad oil in containers contaminates paper and plastic recycling bales at processing plants.

Solution: A quick rinse and drip-dry is all that is needed. Containers do not need to be dishwasher-clean, just food-free.
MISTAKE 02

Bagging Recyclables in Plastic Bags

Thin plastic carrier bags get tangled in rotating sorting gears at material recovery facilities (MRFs), causing facility shutdowns.

Solution: Empty recyclables loose into the blue bin. Return soft carry bags to grocery supermarket drop-off bins.
MISTAKE 03

Mixing Batteries into Normal Trash

Compacted lithium batteries puncture easily inside garbage trucks and landfills, causing toxic fires and releasing neurotoxic cadmium.

Solution: Store dead batteries in a glass jar and drop them at specialized retail collection kiosks.
MISTAKE 04

Recycling Oil-Soaked Pizza Boxes

Grease cannot be washed out with water during paper pulping. The oil forms spots that ruin newly manufactured paper rolls.

Solution: Tear off the clean top lid for paper recycling; compost or trash the greasy base.
MISTAKE 05

Mixing Broken Glass with Bottle Glass

Window panes, light bulbs, and cookware pyrex have different chemical melting points than soda bottle glass, ruining molten glass batches.

Solution: Wrap broken dishware safely in newspaper and dispose in general municipal trash.
MISTAKE 06

Flushing Expired Medicines Down Drains

Water treatment plants cannot filter out antibiotics and hormonal pharmaceuticals, causing antibiotic resistance in rivers.

Solution: Return expired medicines to certified pharmacy take-back drop boxes.

HOME COMPOSTING GUIDE: BEGINNER'S BLUEPRINT

Transform kitchen organic waste into rich black humus right on your balcony or backyard.

STEP 01

Choose Your Setup

Select an aerated compost bin, terracotta pot stack (khamba), or outdoor pit. Ensure it has bottom drainage and aeration holes.

STEP 02

Greens & Browns Ratio

Maintain roughly 2 parts carbon-rich "Browns" for every 1 part nitrogen-rich "Greens" to prevent foul odors and accelerate breakdown.

STEP 03

Aeration & Moisture

Turn the compost weekly with a garden fork. Keep moisture balanced like a wrung-out damp sponge. Harvest crumbly humus in 6–10 weeks.

✓ What Can Go In (Feedstock)

Nitrogen "Greens": Fruit peels, vegetable trimmings, coffee grounds, tea bags (staple-free), crushed eggshells.
Carbon "Browns": Dried leaves, shredded unbleached cardboard, egg cartons, wood shavings, dry coco-peat.

✕ What Must Stay Out

Cooked Meats & Bones: Attract rodents, maggots, and cause severe putrefactive odors.
Dairy & Oily Gravies: Coat feedstock in lipids, blocking oxygen exchange and slowing decomposition.

THE ECOLOGICAL CRISIS

THE REAL PROBLEM WITH WASTE

Municipal solid waste is growing at unprecedented rates, but the primary failure of modern recycling is not technological—it is behavioral and informational at the point of origin.

⚠

Massive Landfill Diversion Failure

Globally, over 2.01 billion tonnes of municipal solid waste are generated annually, with at least 33% not managed in an environmentally safe manner. Valuable recyclables sit buried under tons of mixed garbage.

$

The Contamination Domino Effect

When a single unwashed food container or liquid residue enters a recycling stream, entire bales of cardboard and plastic are rendered unprocessable. Contamination rates routinely exceed 25%.

⏱

Why Sorting at Source is Essential

Mechanical sorting facilities post-collection consume massive electricity and capital. Sorting at the exact moment of disposal is 10x more cost-efficient and preserves pure material quality.
